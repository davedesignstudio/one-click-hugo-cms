Picking the right hardwood is one of the most important decisions in any carpentry project. The species you choose affects durability, appearance, workability, and cost. Here's a practical guide to the woods we use most often at Anthem Carpentry.

## White oak

White oak is our go-to for kitchen cabinets, built-ins, and flooring. It's hard, stable, and takes stain beautifully. The prominent grain pattern adds warmth and character. It handles moisture better than most hardwoods, which makes it ideal for kitchens and bathrooms.

## Hard maple

Maple is pale, tight-grained, and incredibly durable. It's a favorite for cutting boards, butcher blocks, and modern cabinetry where you want a clean, light look. It can be tricky to stain evenly, so we often finish it clear or with a light natural oil.

## Walnut

Walnut is prized for its rich chocolate tones and straight grain. It's softer than oak or maple but machines beautifully and needs little enhancement. We use it for accent pieces, furniture, and statement cabinetry where the wood itself is the design.

## Cherry

Cherry darkens naturally over time, developing a deep reddish patina that many clients love. It's a medium-hard wood that works well for traditional-style furniture and trim. Plan ahead — what you see on day one won't be what you see in five years.

## Three tips before you decide

1. **Consider the room's light.** Dark woods like walnut can make a small room feel smaller; lighter species open things up.
2. **Think about wear.** High-traffic areas benefit from harder species like oak and maple.
3. **See samples in your space.** Wood looks different under natural light, incandescent bulbs, and LEDs. Always test before committing.
