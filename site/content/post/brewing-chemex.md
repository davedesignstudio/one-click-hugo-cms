Custom cabinetry works best when a few decisions are clear early. You don’t need
shop drawings on day one, but knowing how the room is used—and what “finished”
means to you—saves time and protects the budget.

## Start with use, not style

Tell us what gets stored, how doors should open in a tight hall, and whether
you want display or quiet concealment. Style follows function; Pinterest boards
help later.

## Species, finish, and hardware

Walnut and white oak behave differently under oil. Soft-close hinges change the
feel of a kitchen. We’ll bring samples, but come with preferences if you have
them—warm vs cool, matte vs sheen, brass vs black.

## Site realities

Out-of-plumb walls and uneven floors are normal. We plan reveals and scribe
strips so the finished run looks intentional. A site measure is part of every
built-in project for that reason.

## Timeline honesty

Solid wood work isn’t next-week work. Design, milling, assembly, finish, and
install each need room. We’ll give you a schedule we can keep—and update you
if lumber lead times shift.
