This week we’ll walk through the steps we use in the Anthem shop to cut through-dovetails for drawer boxes. The joint is older than power tools and still one of the clearest signs that a piece was made to last.

Designers at museums and craft schools keep returning to dovetails because the geometry is honest: wide pins or narrow pins, the strength is visible and the fit is either right or it isn’t.

## The little secrets of clean dovetails

We cut pins or tails first depending on the job, but the habits that matter most are the same:

1. Always use a sharp marking knife — pencils hide error.
2. Saw to the line, then leave the line.
3. Pare with a chisel; don’t force a proud joint.
4. Dry-fit before glue, every time.

The most distinctive part of a good dovetail isn’t the angle — it’s even baselines, crisp shoulders, and gaps you can’t see without looking for them. Take your time on the first corner; the rest of the box follows that standard.

For a joint that has been used for centuries, dovetails still feel modern when the wood is chosen well and the finish lets the end grain read clearly.
