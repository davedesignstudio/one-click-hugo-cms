The wood you choose for cabinetry shapes more than the look of the room. It affects how doors hang over seasons, how edges take daily wear, and how refinishing will go a decade from now.

## Start with how the kitchen is used

High-traffic kitchens reward denser species. Hard maple and white oak resist denting on drawer fronts and island edges. Walnut and cherry mark more easily but reward careful owners with rich color that deepens under oil finishes.

## Grain and figure

Straight-grained oak reads crisp in modern kitchens and takes stain evenly. Maple stays quiet and bright—ideal when you want the hardware and stone to lead. Walnut’s chocolate tones pair well with brass and stone that has warmth in it.

## Moisture and movement

Solid wood moves across the grain as humidity changes. Good cabinetry accounts for that in panel construction and door design. Ask how floating panels, cross-grain details, and finish schedules are handled before you lock a species.

## Finish compatibility

Oil finishes deepen color and invite touch-up. Film finishes offer more protection against splashes but can look flatter and are harder to spot-repair. Match the finish system to how hard the surface will work.

If you’re weighing options for a remodel, bring samples into the room at different times of day. Light changes everything—and so does the countertop next to it.
