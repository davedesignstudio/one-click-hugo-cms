Picking boards is half the design. A beautiful sketch fails if the lumber fights the form.

## What we look for

Straight grain for legs and rails. Bookmatched pairs for tabletops and door panels. Boards that already feel calm—not stressed with wild reaction wood.

## Matching a room

We bring samples into the client’s light. North windows cool the tone; warm bulbs pull reds in cherry and browns in walnut. Finish samples next to existing floors prevent surprises.

>The best board for a project is often the quiet one—the piece that will still look right in ten years.

Cupping, checking, and pith are deal-breakers for furniture stock. We’d rather wait for the next delivery than build around a compromise.
